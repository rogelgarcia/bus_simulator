Independent controls for different jobs.

  1. Bending: open Branch shape to move the curved zone, adjust its length, and counteract part of the turn near the tip. Shapes emerge from continuous settings. Zero strength keeps it straight.
  2. Placement: side shoots attach at nodes along the actual curve and inherit its local direction.
  3. Thickness: set the base diameter, taper along the wood, and separate thickness losses for side shoots and continuations.
  4. Branching: an editable tree-type range chooses children per parent, or use manual per-level counts. Minimum sizes and bud survival limit fine growth.

Exports contain the actual curves, attachment positions, per-point radii and all settings. This is a structural prototype; the controls are not a full biological simulation.

Species functions · proposed, not implemented

A preset will eventually be able to supply a function for a feature instead of a number. The current shared core already evaluates numeric profiles by crown height, branch role, order and parent attachment position. A future provider could replace a numeric profile. Values along the branch, such as taper, need a separate axis-position input.

When a function supplies a feature, its numeric editor will be disabled and show “Controlled by script for American elm”, with a short rule explanation. Functions will be available only through versioned presets; the UI will not edit or replace them. All controls in this version use editable numeric settings in the shared core.

Planned stages: initial stems, bud placement, bud survival, child dimensions, growth direction, curvature, taper and later leaf placement. Exports will record preset and function versions plus the seed. The full proposed contract is documented in the repository specification.

SpeedTree's parent and profile curves, interval generation, and relative branch dimensions and slenderness pruning provide useful design precedents. Our proposed species-function interface is separate and is not a claim about SpeedTree's plugin API.

What kinds of bending do branches have?

The useful distinction is the process that changes curvature. A letter-like outline can result from several processes acting together. This prototype exposes geometric controls for exploring those outcomes; it does not simulate growth over time or solve elastic forces.

ProcessEffect on an axisMapping to this study
Growth toward light or a preferred gravity angleGradual reorientation of a growing shoot; the response can occupy a broad or localized curved zone.Bend strength, position, zone length and directional bias. Light sensing and a gravity setpoint are not yet solved.
Active straighteningCounteracts accumulated curvature; a distal region may straighten while another region remains bent.Tip recovery counteracts a fraction of the earlier turn. This is a geometric analogue, not the physiological feedback equation.
Mechanical sag under loadWeight bends an axis according to load, geometry and stiffness; this differs from growth reorientation.Not simulated. A future mechanics model needs wood stiffness, taper, and downstream branch and leaf loads.
Reaction wood and retained growth historyWoody axes can adjust posture through new wood formation and retain earlier curvature.Not simulated. Would require growth steps and changing section properties, rather than random point displacement.
Contact with an obstaclePhysical constraints can deflect an axis.Not simulated. The crossing checkbox only rejects planar candidates.

Bastien, Douady & Moulia (2015) combine light response, gravity response and curvature sensing. Coutand, Fournier & Moulia (2007) measure curving and subsequent straightening in woody poplar trunks. Jirasek et al. (2000) integrate mechanical loading, growth responses and contact into procedural plant models.

Modeling decision: preserve relatively straight wood between meaningful changes of direction. A continuing path switching to a daughter branch belongs to the branching graph; it should not be faked by repeatedly bending an uninterrupted axis.

Other architecture properties

Plant architecture distinguishes the stem's continuing tip, lateral buds, nodes and the lengths between nodes. This lets us model different growth roles instead of making every junction equivalent. Pałubicki et al., 2009, §§3–4.

Continuing axis & lateral budsMain-branch mode; separate leader and side lengths, angles and thickness losses.Replacement of a stopped leader by a lateral bud; age-dependent apical control.

Procedural tools also separate branch placement from branch shape: SpeedTree's interval generation exposes frequency, count and spread, while its branch generator separates spine shape, radius and taper. The controls here are our implementation, not copied software.

For a later physical bending model, Jirasek, Prusinkiewicz & Moulia, 2000 integrates gravity, tropisms and contact. The current curvature controls do not solve that mechanics model.
